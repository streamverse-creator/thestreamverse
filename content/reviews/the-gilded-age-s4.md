'The Gilded Age’ returns for its fourth season with a lot happening among New York's wealthy families. The first episode picks up a few months after the previous season, and the characters are already dealing with new complications. Gladys faces a difficult childbirth, Oscar's marriage takes several people by surprise, Agnes is planning her next social move, and Marian and Larry find themselves spending time together again. There is a lot packed into the opening episode, but thankfully these different storylines never confuse viewers. They fit together without making anything rushed. Even in its fourth season, the show hasn't lost its knack for making the personal affairs of wealthy families thoroughly entertaining.

 

This time, the plot begins in England with Gladys Russell (Taissa Farmiga), now the Duchess of Buckingham, facing a dangerous childbirth, with her mother, Bertha (Carrie Coon) by her side. Back in New York, Oscar van Rhijn (Blake Ritson) marries Enid Winterton (Kelley Curran), a union that surprises several guests. Agnes (Christine Baranski) is busy organising a dinner party, hoping to regain some of the standing she has lost. Peggy Scott (Denee Benton) reaches an important point in her personal and professional life. Marian Brook (Louisa Jacobson) and Larry Russell (Harry Richardson) meet again at the wedding, but a storm brings them together for an unexpected night. George Russell (Morgan Spector), meanwhile, is still dealing with the aftermath of the attack on him. 

 

Gladys' difficult childbirth brings a genuine sense of concern, while Oscar and Enid's wedding offers the sort of social intrigue that has always been a major part of the series and adds momentum to the story. Their marriage is particularly interesting because both characters have ambitions of their own, and it remains to be seen how well those ambitions will fit together in future episodes. Bertha's situation is another highlight. A woman who usually knows exactly how to get what she wants is now watching helplessly as her daughter goes through a life-threatening ordeal. George's difficulties also ensure that the attack from the previous season has consequences beyond the immediate incident. There is romance, family tension and plenty of social manoeuvring.

 

Carrie Coon shines once again and gets some of the episode's finest moments as Bertha. Her concern for Gladys reveals a vulnerability that is rarely visible beneath the character's usual determination. Taissa Farmiga makes Gladys's ordeal convincing without overdoing the distress. Blake Ritson and Kelley Curran bring an enjoyable spark to Oscar and Enid's scenes together, making their marriage one of the more interesting developments in the episode. Louisa Jacobson and Harry Richardson are comfortable with each other as Marian and Larry, and their reunion feels believable. Christine Baranski continues to be a scene-stealer as Agnes. Her sharp remarks and impeccable comic timing are among the highlights of the series. The actors make it easy to keep track of the many relationships and rivalries unfolding around them.

 

The fourth season begins on a promising note, and viewers can expect another engaging season. There are enough developments to keep the various storylines moving, and the episode leaves several interesting questions hanging over the characters. Will Oscar and Enid's marriage work in their favour? Can Agnes win back her place in society? And how much more does George have to deal with following the attack? The elaborate costumes, grand interiors and period detailing remain a treat, but it is the characters, their ambitions and their complicated relationships that keep the series interesting.
